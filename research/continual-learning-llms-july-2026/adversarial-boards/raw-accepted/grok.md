# Q1 — Operational scope, taxonomy, and demonstrated evidence

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer

Use two labels: **parametric continual learning** (durable weight/adapter mutation) and **system-level continual adaptation** (durable mutation anywhere in the deployable system). A publishable learning claim requires an authorized, attributable state change from an ordered experience stream that persists beyond the request and yields reproducible held-out gains over matched **reset/stateless** and **frozen full-context** controls. Public evidence shows bounded successes and diagnostic failures; it does **not** show an indefinitely learning frontier system with bounded resources, durable acquisition, safety retention, provenance, privacy, derivative-aware deletion, and coherent whole-bundle rollback.

Primary anchors:  
https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1551/ · https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a774503daed55eb53c634847ae071ec7-Paper-Conference.pdf · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00368 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11020 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07847

### 2. Claims to retain / narrow / downgrade / delete

| Action | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | Two-label taxonomy; durable attributable state; complete system manifests; mandatory reset/full-context controls; access-vs-erasure diagnostics |
| **Narrow** | Single-study “demonstrated” findings to provisional, protocol-scoped evidence |
| **Downgrade** | “No integrated system exists” → “No **publicly reviewed** system jointly demonstrates the full assurance bundle” |
| **Delete** | Weights-only definitions; request-local ICL as durable learning; any single benchmark as deployment proof; five-contender agreement as replication |

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors

- **Category error:** Treating “adaptation,” “access,” “storage,” and “weight learning” as one phenomenon.  
- **Assumption:** That a reconstructible production reset counterfactual exists when environment, tools, and corpora drift.  
- **Assumption:** That “trajectory-native” evaluation is always cheaper than wrong architecture—true only if the metric set is pruned.  
- **Echo risk:** Contenders agree on definitions because they shared a pack, not because the field independently converged.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal

**Counter:** Broadening CL to system-level state dilutes the scientific target and lets product teams rebrand RAG as continual learning.  
**Rebuttal:** The risk is real—and is controlled by **strict admission criteria** (durable state, held-out gain, matched controls, revocation class), not by a weights-only gate that erases the actual deployment surface. Restrictive taxonomy without those gates still fails: teams ship durable memory and call it “not CL.”

### 5. Hardened answer (publication-ready)

Continual learning claims must declare substrate and complete signed system manifest; stream regime and identity assumptions; experience trust and admission status; valid-time/transaction-time semantics; retention, transfer, recovery, and reversibility class; training, inference, and persistent-state budgets; and safety/privacy/deletion/rollback obligations. Parametric CL and system-level continual adaptation are distinct labels. Re-supplied request context is not durable learning. Persistent retrieval, summaries, or memory may count as system-level adaptation only when they produce durable held-out gains over matched reset/stateless and frozen full-context controls, and must not be sold as equivalent to weight learning. Public evidence supports protocol-scoped successes (e.g., replay/scheduling in web pretraining; recoverable decline diagnostics; loss without free-form recall; weak unprompted retention after sequential factual writes; mixed memory vs context/RAG results). It does not demonstrate an indefinitely learning frontier system that jointly satisfies bounded resources and the full assurance bundle.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope inflation: retrieval/prompting sold as learning | Claimed gain vanishes under reset or matched frozen full-context | High | High | Require durable attributable state, cross-session held-out gain, substrate tags, named revocation | Research Definition Lead |
| Over-strict reversibility kills exploratory science | Promising arm rejected solely for missing production lineage tooling | Medium | Medium | Separate sandbox evidence from release eligibility | Scientific Programme Director |
| Shared-pack agreement treated as replication | Leadership cites five-contender agreement as empirical convergence | High | High | Label shared-context synthesis explicitly; require independent implementations before promotion | Evidence Governance Lead |

### 7. Confidence: **8/10**

High on the definitional boundary and evidence-ceiling claim (supported by multiple primary negative/diagnostic results). Lower on production reconstruction of reset counterfactuals and private-system absence claims.

### 8. Recommendation: **Conditional Go**

Adopt the taxonomy and claim gates for all publication and funding language. Do not treat definitional cleanup as solved architecture.

### 9. Decisive experiment + stop condition

**Experiment:** Long randomized mixed streams; systems labeled “continual” vs reset/stateless, frozen full-context, temporal-RAG, and periodic-retraining controls at matched budgets.  
**Stop/falsifier:** Systems labeled continual fail to produce durable held-out gains over those controls (or gains are fully explained by re-supplied context).

# Q2 — Plasticity, forgetting, and interference control

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer

No validated universal ordering exists among replay, importance regularization, orthogonal/sparse adapters, expansion, editing, dense tuning, and continual policy optimization. Plasticity should be a **constraint-keyed governed update service**: immutable base checkpoint, quarantined candidates, lawful provenance-bearing replay, revocable adapters/sparse regions, capacity/routing registry, release controller. Replay is the mandatory **lawful-retention reference arm**, not a universal winner. Replay **rate ≠ retained corpus size**. Plasticity loss is demonstrated only at 5M–314M non-embedding parameters (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24752). Every parameter update suspends inherited safety claims until re-qualification.

Primary anchors:  
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01908 · https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.715/ · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24752 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05683 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15053

### 2. Claims to retain / narrow / downgrade / delete

| Action | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | No universal mechanism ranking; replay as reference arm; post-update re-qualification; complete rollback; least-irreversible preference as **policy** |
| **Narrow** | Orthogonal/sparse/editing benefits to finite protocols and evaluated methods |
| **Downgrade** | “Least irreversible first is empirically proven” → safety-motivated policy under uncertainty |
| **Delete** | Replay as always best/always mandatory; fixed replay percentages; O-LoRA near-zero BWT as generic; TFGN near-zero BWT as settled; plasticity-loss → frontier; CIFAR/MNIST → LLM guarantees |

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors

- **Category error:** Pooling BWT/forgetting percentages across incompatible streams, models, and metrics.  
- **Assumption:** That “constraint-keyed selection” is more than a decision-tree branding of ignorance.  
- **Assumption:** That modular isolation **prevents** saturation rather than **defers** it into routing/growth costs.  
- **Echo risk:** Contenders “refuse to rank” because ranking is hard—and then treat refusal as a positive scientific result.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal

**Counter:** A pure replay + dense CPT recipe (TiC-LM-style) already works at web scale; governance service is over-engineering.  
**Rebuttal:** TiC-LM shows **comparable general Common-Crawl held-out loss** at ~2.6× less compute in a 1B/3B protocol (https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1551/), not capability/safety/calibration parity or deletion-ready deployment. Replay also creates privacy, poison, and deletion obligations that pure “it works” recipes ignore.

### 5. Hardened answer (publication-ready)

Operate every update as a governed transaction selecting among no-update, external state, modular update, sparse protected update, or offline dense consolidation based on stream/knowledge type, reuse, lawful retention/deletion, interference and safety drift, capacity/latency/serving cost, attribution/reversibility, and total assurance cost. Replay is the strongest **reference baseline when lawful retention is allowed**; it is not a universal winner after privacy, storage, and task-boundary constraints. Orthogonal and sparse methods report finite-protocol benefits; expansion trades interference for growth and routing; editors evaluated in WikiBigEdit degrade over long sequences (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05683)—this bounds tested editors, not all future editors. Importance regularization has narrow positive LLM evidence (e.g., Gemma 2 language adaptation: https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05946), not a general ordering. Plasticity loss at tested mid-scales does not establish frontier behavior. Dense base-weight consolidation must be offline, rebuildable, lineage-complete, and covered by atomic whole-system rollback.

### 6. Three risks

| Risk | Trigger | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Replay retains poison/sensitive/restricted data | Replay item fails consent, expiry, poison, or deletion audit | High | High | Govern replay as persistent state; purpose/tenant keys; rebuild alternative | Data Governance Owner |
| Protected modules defer interference into unbounded growth | Persistent bytes, router error, or retirement backlog breaches envelope | Medium-High | High | Capacity registry, growth caps, retirement drills, periodic-rebuild comparison | Plasticity Engineering Lead |
| Weight updates silently erode safety/calibration/grounding | Post-update assurance metric worsens beyond bound while accuracy rises | High | Critical | Never inherit safety evidence; full re-qualification; restore prior bundle on failure | Independent Safety Lead |

### 7. Confidence: **6/10** on decision procedure; **3/10** on any global ranking

The “don’t rank incompatible papers” conclusion is solid. The “governed update service” is prudent engineering, not an experimentally established optimum. VC lens: this is process spend without a priced win condition unless F4 is run.

### 8. Recommendation: **Conditional Go** (procedure only)

Authorize constraint-keyed release policy and replay-as-reference. **No-Go** on crowning any anti-forgetting stack or funding expansion/editing as default.

### 9. Decisive experiment + stop condition

**Experiment:** Preregistered multi-family matched-resource tournament across stream types, replay-lawful and replay-prohibited regimes, randomized orders, independent harnesses.  
**Stop/falsifier:** One stable mechanism ordering emerges across families/regimes **or** a simple fixed mechanism dominates all regimes—either outcome collapses the “constraint-keyed service” thesis into a simpler rule.

# Q3 — Memory, retrieval, adapters, experts, editing, and consolidation

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer

Heterogeneous placement is a **falsifiable risk-control hypothesis**, not architecture doctrine. Direct comparative support covers request context as control, trusted versioned temporal retrieval vs several editors (https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.546/), and one scoped persistent-feedback memory result (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22157). General episodic memory, signed skills/adapters, sparse modules, and broad consolidation remain **unpriced**. External memory relocates interference (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27003); CL-Bench challenges automatic memory benefit (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661).

### 2. Claims to retain / narrow / downgrade / delete

| Action | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | Placement axes; promotion/demotion; derivative lineage; whole-bundle rollback; interference relocation |
| **Narrow** | Six-tier stack → minimum testable hypothesis with only context, temporal retrieval, and one feedback-memory result having direct comparative support |
| **Downgrade** | Temporal RAG as “engineering default” → provisional control with scoped comparative evidence |
| **Delete** | Hierarchy-as-winner; external memory as intrinsically safe/fully reversible; ChronoMem as deletion closure; CL-Bench as support for hierarchy; author-asserted “first” priority |

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors

- **Category error:** Exporting a risk-control hypothesis into “standing architecture posture.”  
- **Assumption:** That more tiers increase optionality without increasing routing, deletion, and ops surface.  
- **Assumption:** That promotion criteria (reuse, shadow eval, lineage) can be measured without gaming.  
- **Echo risk:** All contenders restate the hierarchy because the pack primes it—Medium contradiction matrix correctly flags this as weakly evidenced consensus.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal

**Counter:** Build the full hierarchy now; ablations can prune later.  
**Rebuttal:** Infrastructure lock-in is the default failure mode of systems programmes. Each tier creates derivative surfaces (embeddings, summaries, adapters, router policies). F1 is the only cheap kill. Funding multi-site hierarchy before subtraction is a capital-allocation error, not “optionality.”

### 5. Hardened answer (publication-ready)

Place state by mutability, attribution, reuse, latency, lifecycle cost, and required reversibility—not by aesthetic multi-timescale biology analogies. Prefer versioned temporal retrieval for mutable facts when a trusted temporal store exists; treat additional tiers as ablatable hypotheses that must beat temporal RAG and governed periodic rebuild under matched inference budget, persistent bytes, and full lifecycle cost. Promotion requires repeated validated reuse, shadow benefit, complete lineage, lifecycle savings, and a passed revocation drill. Contradiction, poison, declining reuse, routing failure, or assurance regression triggers quarantine/demotion/retirement. Rollback covers the entire deployable bundle. ChronoMem (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27773) demonstrates rollback machinery, not exhaustive derivative deletion.

### 6. Three risks

| Risk | Trigger | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy becomes architecture lock-in before pricing | Multi-site/production infra funded before F1 | Medium | High | Enforce F1 first gate; delete non-load-bearing tiers | Portfolio Controller |
| Learned/implicit routing selects stale/poisoned/wrong-tenant state | Route-conditioned failures or silent unsafe routes exceed bound | Medium-High | Critical | Valid-time/tenant checks, abstention, explicit fallback, stop authority | Routing and Isolation Lead |
| Derivative proliferation defeats deletion/rollback economics | Unmapped embeddings/summaries/caches/adapters/weights in audit | High | High | Content-addressed derivative graph; rebuildable epochs; adversarial closure tests | State Lifecycle Owner |

### 7. Confidence: **5/10** on placement logic; **3/10** on integrated hierarchy

Placement axes are sensible. The integrated stack is the programme’s largest unpriced bet. Claude’s Q3 dissent is the sharpest funding objection in the pack.

### 8. Recommendation: **Conditional Go** for F1 only; **No-Go** for hierarchy as architecture

### 9. Decisive experiment + stop condition

**Experiment:** Single-site subtractive ablation (full min stack; −episodic; −skills/adapters; −sparse; −consolidation; temporal-RAG-only; periodic rebuild); predeclare predicted load-bearing tier.  
**Stop/falsifier:** Removing unevidenced tiers causes no reproducible loss, or temporal RAG / periodic rebuild matches the full stack at lower lifecycle cost.

# Q4 — Experience admission, governance, privacy, provenance, deletion, and rollback

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer

Deployment experience is untrusted evidence. Durable mutation requires a liability-controlled admission/release pipeline (capture → classify → quarantine/screen → corroborate → shadow-test → least-irreversible substrate → signed manifest release → lineage/monitoring/rollback/qualified receipts). Provenance is origin history, not truth. Deletion closure is a **testable coverage gate under residual-risk language**, not an achieved universal property. Rollback = coherent restoration of the complete deployable bundle.

Primary anchors:  
https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7e810b2c75d69be186cadd2fe3febeab-Abstract-Conference.html · https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784 · https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05012 · https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/index.html · https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_17/oj/eng · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04379 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27080

### 2. Claims to retain / narrow / downgrade / delete

| Action | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | Pre-mutation admission; least-irreversible placement; signed manifests; dependency lineage; qualified receipts; atomic bundle rollback |
| **Narrow** | Deletion closure → measured coverage + explicit residual risk; intake prevention → preferred hypothesis under uncertainty |
| **Downgrade** | C2PA to one provenance input among many |
| **Delete** | Perfect/certified deletion; technical unlearning as automatic GDPR satisfaction; provenance-as-truth; semantic rollback or registry alias as full-system rollback; scale-independent poison rates; exact unsupported episode/latency/compute thresholds |

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors

- **Category error:** Equating legal erasure, source deletion, semantic rollback, behavioral unavailability, full-bundle restore, and causal influence removal.  
- **Assumption:** That governance overhead is always justified by risk reduction (unpriced).  
- **Assumption:** That static non-adaptive poison studies predict adaptive attackers.  
- **Regulator trap:** Overclaiming technical compliance creates false assurance and liability.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal

**Counter:** Skip heavy admission; rebuild periodically from a curated corpus and get deletion/rollback “for free.”  
**Rebuttal:** This is the strongest cheap substitute and may win. The programme must treat rebuild as a first-class arm, not a strawman. Admission is justified only if it reduces attack consequence and residual influence enough to beat rebuild’s utility/cost profile (F5).

### 5. Hardened answer (publication-ready)

Make admission the governance atom for every durable mutation. Screen for poison, conflict, duplication, privacy exposure, and unverifiable feedback; require corroboration or executable verification before shared promotion; shadow-test utility, calibration, grounding, safety, privacy, and tenant isolation. Provenance asserts claimed origin/history only (C2PA-style assertions do not establish truth: https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/index.html). Deletion receipts must state what was removed, rebuilt, disabled, made inaccessible, or retained as residual risk across sources, embeddings, indexes, summaries, caches, logs, replay corpora, adapters, router policies, and consolidated weights. Machine unlearning can fail to remove poisoning effects under tested methods (ICLR 2025). Behavioral unavailability is not causal erasure. GDPR Art. 17 obligations require legal analysis and are not satisfied by a single technical probe.

### 6. Three risks

| Risk | Trigger | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive attackers learn admission thresholds | Quarantined item reaches serving or unauthorized shared promotion | Medium-High | Critical | Global stop; adaptive red team; corroboration; canaries; independent quarantine authority | Security Red-Team Lead |
| Governance latency kills legitimate learning | Review queues breach SLAs or useful-event rejection rises materially | High | Medium-High | Risk-tiered review; audited automation; least-irreversible temp placement; rebuild comparison | Experience Operations Lead |
| Deletion receipts overstate removal/compliance | Completed receipt coexists with known recoverable derivative or unsupported legal language | Medium | Critical | Qualified vocabulary; separate residual classes; dual legal+technical sign-off | Privacy Counsel and Deletion Lead |

### 7. Confidence: **7/10** on governance requirements; **3/10** on scalable deletion certification

Requirements are the right regulatory/engineering floor. Certifiable scalable closure is not in the evidence.

### 8. Recommendation: **Conditional Go** for admission + lineage + qualified receipts; **No-Go** for “complete deletion” product claims

### 9. Decisive experiment + stop condition

**Experiment:** F5/F11 style: governed admission vs direct ingest vs direct+repair vs periodic rebuild under poison, consent withdrawal, and derivative canaries.  
**Stop/falsifier:** A completed deletion receipt issues while a known derivative remains recoverable/activatable; or rebuild matches utility and attack consequence at lower cost.

# Q5 — Longitudinal evaluation, safety retention, and assurance

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer

Evaluate a **trajectory of complete system manifests**, not a final checkpoint. Endpoint accuracy cannot distinguish acquisition failure, destructive interference, recoverable access loss, retrieval failure, grounding drift, calibration collapse, policy refusal, or plasticity loss. Mandatory controls: reset/stateless, frozen full-context, strong temporal RAG, periodic retraining. Safety/privacy/tenant isolation/deletion are non-compensable gates. Metric-retention rule: keep metrics only if they detect distinct seeded failures or change decisions.

Primary anchors:  
https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a774503daed55eb53c634847ae071ec7-Paper-Conference.pdf · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00368 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23987 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02020 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29914 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20961

### 2. Claims to retain / narrow / downgrade / delete

| Action | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | Trajectory manifests; prequential+retrospective testing; protocol tuples; mandatory controls; independent-harness audit; governed evaluation logs |
| **Narrow** | Expanded metric catalogue → empirically retained minimal set |
| **Downgrade** | Multi-site reproduction → required for load-bearing claims only |
| **Delete** | Final-score sufficiency; universal pass thresholds; composite scores without justified normalization; pooling numeric ranges across dissimilar regimes |

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors

- **Category error:** Treating harness-sensitive rank reversals as universal method orderings.  
- **Assumption:** That more metrics always mean more truth rather than more theatre and more retained sensitive logs.  
- **Assumption:** That contamination resistance can be maintained on long-lived public evals.  
- **VC risk:** Evaluation platforms become the product while architecture decisions never get killed.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal

**Counter:** Trajectory-native full-manifest eval is too expensive; industry will keep using endpoint scores.  
**Rebuttal:** Cost is real—so F2 must prune. But endpoint-only ranking is already shown to miss grounding/calibration/recoverability failures in primary diagnostics. The cheap wrong answer is still wrong; fund metric pruning, not metric maximalism.

### 5. Hardened answer (publication-ready)

Publish protocol tuples: stream, order, identity assumptions, substrate manifest, compute/inference/state budgets, controls, and gates. Measure acquisition, transfer, max degradation, recoverability/recovery cost, fixed-budget future plasticity, calibration/coverage/abstention/grounding, temporal consistency/staleness, safety/privacy/tool-policy/tenant isolation, routing and poison persistence, state growth and lifecycle costs, deletion/rollback drills, and rejected-release/incident costs—**only while each metric earns its keep under the retention rule**. Match resources where feasible; report routing, retirement, evaluation, deletion, and incident costs separately. Utility may not compensate for assurance-gate failure. Evaluation logs are governed retained state.

| Evaluation logs become privacy/poison substrate | Logs hold undeclared sensitive content, survive deletion, or leak into training | Medium | High | Govern logs as state; strict separation; retention limits; include in closure drills | Evaluation Data Custodian |

### 7. Confidence: **8/10** on trajectory-native necessity; **4/10** on any minimal metric set or threshold

### 8. Recommendation: **Go** for trajectory protocol + metric pruning; **No-Go** for universal thresholds or composite leaderboards

### 9. Decisive experiment + stop condition

**Experiment:** Same systems and seeded faults under endpoint-only vs conventional BWT/FWT vs full-manifest protocols.

## Red-team review: privacy, poisoning, provenance, deletion, rollback, safety-retention

### Privacy
- **Attack:** Experience streams, replay buffers, eval logs, and feedback memories become silent PII/sensitive stores.  
- **Finding:** Programme correctly flags logs and replay as retained state; still under-prices **embedding inversion, summary leakage, and cross-session linkage**.  
- **Control bar:** Purpose limitation; tenant namespaces; synthetic/consent-cleared pilots only; no real PII in F1; privacy regression as non-compensable gate.  
- **Kill:** Any cross-tenant disclosure or undeclared sensitive retention in eval logs.

### Poisoning
- **Attack:** AgentPoison / PoisonedRAG / KB poisoning class threats (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07867, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04379); unlearning may not remove poison (ICLR 2025 unlearning paper).  
- **Finding:** Contender consensus prefers intake prevention—reasonable under uncertainty, **not** empirically proven superior to rebuild. Adaptive attackers will optimize against static thresholds.  
- **Control bar:** Quarantine, canaries, corroboration, executable verification, rotating red-team, immediate global stop on escape.  
- **Kill:** Known poison reaches serving; shared promotion without authorization.

### Provenance
- **Attack:** Signed manifests and C2PA-style assertions used as truth/consent/authorization theatre.  
- **Finding:** Correct board position: provenance = claimed history, not truth (https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/index.html).  
- **Control bar:** Separate identity, consent, legal basis, and content integrity fields; never collapse into one signature.  
- **Kill:** Release that treats provenance signature as safety or legality proof.

### Deletion
- **Attack:** Delete source row; retain vectors, caches, traces, adapters, consolidated weights; issue “complete” receipt.  
- **Finding:** Strongest hard problem in the pack. ChronoMem ≠ derivative closure. Legal erasure ≠ causal erasure. CIFAR/MNIST theory does not transfer.  
- **Control bar:** Derivative graph mandatory; receipts enumerate removed/rebuilt/disabled/inaccessible/residual; probes for retrieval/trigger/membership/behavior; residual-risk standard explicit.  
- **Kill:** Completed receipt while known derivative recoverable above residual standard.

### Rollback
- **Attack:** Alias reassignment or memory semantic rollback while indexes, tools, prompts, and policies diverge.  
- **Finding:** Correctly identified as full-bundle problem; still easy to ship as metadata theatre.  
- **Control bar:** Atomic restore of model, adapters, indexes, memories, prompts, tools, router, policy; hash/dependency consistency checks; drill frequency preregistered.  
- **Kill:** Incoherent multi-component restore or inability to restore pre-event bundle in drill.

### Safety retention
- **Attack:** Task accuracy rises while refusal/tool-policy/grounding/calibration degrade after continual updates.  
- **Finding:** Hidden forgetting and calibration-collapse primaries make this credible (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23987). Inherited safety claims after weight mutation are unacceptable.  
- **Control bar:** Safety evidence never inherited across parameter updates; non-compensable gates; canary suites for tool-policy and tenant isolation; hard restore on breach.  
- **Kill:** Unrecoverable safety regression or utility-compensated gate bypass.
