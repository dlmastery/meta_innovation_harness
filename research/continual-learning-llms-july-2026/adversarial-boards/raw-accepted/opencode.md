## Q1 — Operational scope, taxonomy, and demonstrated evidence

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
Two labels — **parametric** (durable weight/adapter mutation) and **system-level** (durable state anywhere: retrieval, episodic memory, prompt state, skills, routers, tools, policy). A publishable learning claim requires an authorized, attributable state change from an ordered experience stream that persists beyond the request and yields reproducible held-out improvement over matched reset/stateless and frozen full-context controls. Request-local context is not durable learning. The public evidence supports bounded successes and diagnostic failures; no reviewed public system jointly provides bounded resources, durable acquisition, safety retention, provenance, privacy, derivative-aware deletion, and whole-bundle rollback.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** two-label taxonomy; complete-signed-manifest obligations; request-local ≠ durable learning; mandatory controls.
- **Narrow:** TiC-LM (https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1551/) to its 1B/3B web-pretraining protocol (~2.6× lower compute for comparable held-out loss) — not general capability, safety, or production parity.
- **Downgrade:** "Beyond Perplexity" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00368) and "Can a Language Model Learn Facts Continually in Its Weights?" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11020) to unreplicated, protocol-scoped 2026 preprints; the factual-write paper is single-author with invented facts in one tested family.
- **Delete:** any reading that the reviewed-literature absence proves no private system exists (retain the scoped qualifier); any single benchmark as deployment proof.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes "ordered experience stream" is definable in production where order, boundaries, and identity assumptions are often unavailable.
- Category error: the binary taxonomy may underrepresent hybrid weight+memory systems that mutate both jointly.
- Central control may be unobtainable: the packet itself lists "how to reconstruct a reliable reset/stateless counterfactual for a production system" as unresolved — the decisive comparator is least available where it matters most.
- Confound control (prompt caching, contamination, eval leakage) is absent from the claim tuple even though held-out gain is the load-bearing signal.
- Category error: "authorized state change" is a governance property, not an epistemic one; authorization and learning are conflated in the definitional sentence.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The evidence bar is so demanding it makes legitimate continual-learning work unevaluable or publishable only post-deploy; and "no indefinitely learning frontier system" is a sweeping negative that a non-exhaustive corpus cannot support — absence of public demonstration is weak evidence of absence.
**Rebuttal:** The bar is epistemic, not bureaucratic: it defines what a claim must show and prevents architecture/funding from resting on single-study evidence. The negative is already scoped to "publicly documented systems in the reviewed literature" (prohibited claim 28). The demanding bar is the intended mechanism, not a bug.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: declare confound controls (caching, contamination, leakage) alongside the protocol tuple; restate the negative with the public-documentation scope in every abstract and press summary.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q1-R1 — Label drift at funding gates.** Trigger: reward announcements favor "continual learning," teams submit memory-harness results labeled as weight learning. Likelihood: High. Impact: High (false capability assurance, misdirected resources). Mitigation: mandatory signed substrate+manifest declaration; independent audit of label–substrate match. Owner: Research lead / controller.
- **Q1-R2 — Unreconstructible reset counterfactual invalidates the control.** Trigger: production dependencies change mid-window; no frozen snapshot. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (decisive falsifier untestable in deployment). Mitigation: preregistered frozen reference environment; periodic-retraining and frozen-full-context substitutes; mandatory counterfactual-reconstruction disclosure. Owner: Evaluation/replication lead.
- **Q1-R3 — Scoped negative quoted as proof-of-absence.** Trigger: press/regulators quote the negative without scope. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium (false assurance or unwarranted private-sector skepticism). Mitigation: keep prohibited-claim 28 in all published summaries; paired scope qualifier. Owner: Communications/releases governance.

### 7. Confidence
**8/10** on the taxonomy and evidence boundary — calibrated as confidence in the definitions being defensible, not in the empirical base; drops toward 7 if production counterfactual reconstruction proves infeasible.

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** — publishable taxonomy with manifest gates; no architecture funding on single-study findings.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
Across long randomized streams, systems labeled continual fail to produce durable held-out gains over matched reset/stateless, frozen full-context, temporal-RAG, and periodic-retraining controls. Stop: on failure, no substrate under test receives architecture investment.

## Q2 — Plasticity, forgetting, and interference control

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
No validated universal ordering exists among replay, regularization, orthogonal/sparse adapters, expansion, editing, dense tuning, and policy optimization. Operate plasticity as a constraint-keyed, governed update service: immutable base checkpoint, quarantined candidate data, provenance-bearing replay, revocable adapters/sparse regions, capacity/routing registry, release controller. Replay is the mandatory lawful-retention reference arm, not a universal winner. Every parameter update suspends inherited safety/assurance claims until re-qualified; dense consolidation is offline, rebuildable, lineage-complete, and covered by atomic whole-bundle rollback.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** refusal to crown a mechanism; replay as reference arm; constraint-keyed selection; post-update re-qualification; complete rollback.
- **Narrow:** least-irreversible-first and weight-mutation-last to safety-motivated policies, not experimentally proven universal orderings; adapter/sparse/consolidation benefits to finite protocols.
- **Downgrade:** importance regularization's one narrow positive LLM adaptation result (single study, unreplicated); "Can Scale Save Us From Plasticity Loss" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24752) scoped to 5M–314M non-embedding parameters.
- **Delete:** CIFAR/MNIST/linear-model results as LLM guarantees; replay sampling rate as replay-corpus size.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes the release controller can measure reuse, acquisition difficulty, and lawful retention pre-hoc — these selection keys are not reliably measurable before the update.
- Category error: treating "least irreversible" as a proxy for "best"; a one-time dense update may be cheaper and safer than a revocable-but-flaky modular path.
- Assumes re-qualification scope follows dependency/risk analysis, but the method for that analysis is itself unvalidated — the packet concedes it is not a "source-proven universal law."
- Category error: replay-as-reference conflates a legal-retention constraint with an empirical evidence baseline.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The no-ordering stance is a failure to conclude. A noisy evidence gradient exists — dense updating shows recurring degradation, orthogonal/sparse shows bounded gains, replay carries the broadest positive evidence — and refusing to rank leaves practitioners with no guidance while the "governed service" is itself an unvalidated mechanism choice.
**Rebuttal:** The gradient is protocol-scoped and contradictory across incompatible studies; ranking it manufactures certainty. The governed-service abstraction converts an unsupported global ordering into a per-update decision procedure under explicit constraints, which is more actionable than a false leaderboard. Matched-budget comparison remains the condition for any ranking.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: every governed update must emit a decision record (stream identity, chosen mechanism, budgets, re-qualification scope, revocation path, rollback receipt) so "governed" is auditable rather than asserted.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q2-R1 — Replay reference arm becomes unlawful retention.** Trigger: replay corpus retains user-derived episodes beyond consent/legal basis. Likelihood: High. Impact: High (deletion/closure violations, regulator action). Mitigation: provenance-bearing replay, retention schedules keyed to legal basis, replay-corpus deletion drills. Owner: Privacy/legal governance.
- **Q2-R2 — Re-qualification becomes rubber-stamp or unbounded cost.** Trigger: no validated scoping method; teams either skip or gold-plate. Likelihood: High. Impact: Medium–High (silent safety drift or stalled releases). Mitigation: preregistered re-qualification decision tree; negative-result publication; re-qualification budgeted in lifecycle cost. Owner: Assurance/release controller.
- **Q2-R3 — Plasticity-loss 5M–314M results extrapolated to frontier systems.** Trigger: abstracts or internal memos generalize the scaling finding to production models. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium (wrong architectural bets on scale-as-shield). Mitigation: enforce prohibited claim 9; cite explicit non-embedding-parameter scope; frontier checkpoints. Owner: Research lead.

### 7. Confidence
**7/10** on the constraint-keyed decision procedure (calibrated as confidence in the epistemic stance, not the service's operational success); **4/10** on any global mechanism ordering — correctly refused.

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** for the governed-update service; **No-Go** for any mechanism tournament until matched budgets and protocol tuples.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
A preregistered, independently reproduced, matched-resource tournament yields one stable mechanism ordering across model families, stream types, replay-lawful and replay-prohibited regimes, randomized orders, and independent harnesses. Stop: if no stable ordering emerges, the "no universal ordering" claim stands and rankings remain prohibited.

## Q3 — Memory, retrieval, adapters, experts, editing, and consolidation

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
Heterogeneous state placement is a falsifiable risk-control hypothesis, not established architecture. Placement is determined by mutability, attribution, reuse, latency, lifecycle cost, and required reversibility. Direct comparative evidence covers only request context (control), trusted versioned temporal retrieval (beats tested editors in its setting), and one scoped persistent-feedback memory. General tiers are unpriced. External memory relocates rather than eliminates interference; deleting a source row does not establish closure. Promotion requires repeated validated reuse, shadow-evaluation benefit, complete lineage, lifecycle savings, and a passed revocation drill.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** placement axes; two-sided promotion/demotion; derivative lineage; whole-bundle rollback; interference-relocation point.
- **Narrow:** the six-tier stack to a minimum testable hypothesis; ChronoMem (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27773) to rollback machinery, not deletion closure.
- **Downgrade:** "Learning on the Job" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22157) to a single-domain, unreplicated July 2026 preprint; "When Continual Learning Moves to Memory" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27003) to work-in-progress.
- **Delete:** hierarchy-as-winner; ChronoMem author-asserted "first" priority; treating Continual Learning Bench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661) as hierarchy support when its result challenges automatic memory benefit.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes tiers are cleanly separable for subtraction; deployed bundles mix tiers and ablation cannot cleanly attribute.
- Category error: placement is decided by routers that are themselves durable learned state — a feedback loop the hierarchy does not model.
- Assumes lifecycle costing is transparent and comparable before the experiment, yet that comparability is exactly what the stack's own cost function lacks.
- Hidden assumption: "repeated validated reuse" presumes reuse is measurable and attributable, requiring instrumentation that may perturb behavior.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The posture embeds an unproven prior dressed as neutrality — temporal retrieval is defaulted to provisional-control status on the strength of one study (Chronos in its trusted temporal KB) while every other tier must earn subtraction evidence; held to the same bar, temporal retrieval is itself "unpriced."
**Rebuttal:** The provisional default is not an empirical winner; it is a conservative tie-breaking rule — the low-irreversibility incumbent control wins ties until a challenger demonstrates marginal value under matched lifecycle costing. That is a decision rule under uncertainty, explicitly not a superiority claim, and it is symmetric in its evidence demands once subtraction runs.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: pre-commit the tie-break rule (lowest-irreversibility adequate substrate wins ties) and publish the subtraction design before results.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q3-R1 — Six-tier architectural lock-in despite "ablatable" framing.** Trigger: teams build all tiers for the ablation and keep them for operational convenience. Likelihood: High. Impact: High (unpriced tiers become standing infrastructure by inertia). Mitigation: fund only the minimum instrumented hierarchy; sunset removed tiers; envelope budget; kill condition keyed to subtraction results. Owner: Programme/architecture lead.
- **Q3-R2 — Incomplete derivative lineage → false deletion closure.** Trigger: an embedding/summary/router derives from a deleted source row and survives. Likelihood: High. Impact: High (regulatory breach). Mitigation: derivative ledger mandatory before promotion; receipts state residual risk; drill-based verification. Owner: Data-governance owner.
- **Q3-R3 — Opaque routing failures from learned placement.** Trigger: misrouting with no attribution; behavior degrades silently. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium–High (silent production misbehavior). Mitigation: route-conditioned failure metrics; explicit policy fallback; router audit trail; canary routing. Owner: Systems/red-team lead.

### 7. Confidence
**6/10** on placement logic (calibrated as confidence in the decision axes); **4/10** on the integrated hierarchy (deliberately low — untested).

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** for one preregistered single-site subtractive ablation; **No-Go** for any multi-site architecture programme before the ablation.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
A temporal-RAG-only or governed periodic-rebuild arm matches the hierarchy on trajectory utility, assurance, reversibility, and lifecycle cost — or removing a proposed tier causes no reproducible loss. Stop: delete every tier without demonstrated marginal value; replicate only survivors.

## Q4 — Experience admission, governance, privacy, provenance, deletion, and rollback

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
Deployment experience is untrusted evidence. Every durable mutation passes a liability-controlled eight-step pipeline: capture, classify, quarantine, corroborate, shadow-test, least-irreversible placement, signed manifest with staged release, and dependency lineage with qualified deletion receipts. Provenance attests origin/history, not truth. Deletion closure is a testable coverage gate under a documented residual-risk standard, not an achieved or certifiable property. Rollback means coherent restoration of the complete deployable bundle.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** pre-mutation admission; least-irreversible placement; signed manifests; dependency lineage; qualified receipts; atomic bundle rollback.
- **Narrow:** deletion closure to measured coverage with explicit residual risk; intake prevention to a preferred hypothesis; C2PA (https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/index.html) to one provenance input.
- **Downgrade:** "Making AI Forget You" (https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05012) to 2019 linear/statistical-model background, not an LLM deletion guarantee.
- **Delete:** perfect deletion; technical unlearning as automatic GDPR Article 17 satisfaction (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/art_17/oj/eng); provenance as truth; registry-alias/semantic rollback as full rollback; scale-independent poison percentages; unsupported exact episode/latency/compute thresholds.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes intake screening withstands adaptive attackers — listed unresolved in the packet; the pipeline is only as strong as its adversary model.
- Category error: treating "documented residual risk" as dischargeable, when legal erasure may not accept a residual-risk receipt.
- Assumes deletion coverage is enumerable; derivatives can be unknown, so a "coverage gate" presumes a closed catalog.
- Hidden assumption: shadow-testing before release catches assurance regressions; shadow tests have their own coverage limits.
- Category error: prevention-at-intake vs repair is a preference under uncertainty, yet the operational default embeds it.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The governance stack will starve the programme of velocity and can become an oracle for adversaries; worse, "deletion closure under documented residual risk" may not satisfy regulators, who demand legal erasure, not a technical ledger — the packet substitutes an engineering artifact for a legal obligation.
**Rebuttal:** The heaviness is proportionate to the liability of mutable deployed state. The packet explicitly refuses to conflate legal and technical erasure (prohibited claim 19) and requires context-specific legal analysis for GDPR Article 17; it does not substitute a ledger for the law. The cost objection is real and is exactly why Q6 gates the programme — governance overhead is priced, not waved away. Prevention-at-intake is honestly labeled a hypothesis, not a proven winner.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: an explicit clause that no admission step is represented as a security guarantee against adaptive adversaries; adversarial resilience of screening is an open research question reported as such.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q4-R1 — Poison persists in derivatives despite intake screening.** Trigger: poisoned episode admitted after screening; backdoor survives in embeddings/replay/adapters. Likelihood: Medium–High. Impact: High (triggered misbehavior). Mitigation: independent detection layer; quarantined intake; periodic post-admission poisoning audits; rollback bundles. Owner: Security/red-team.
- **Q4-R2 — Deletion-coverage gate certified with incomplete derivative catalog.** Trigger: a derivative outside the ledger is recovered post-receipt. Likelihood: High. Impact: High (the Q4 decisive falsifier). Mitigation: receipts state catalog scope and method; adversarial-discovery drills; mandatory residual-risk disclosure. Owner: Privacy/legal.
- **Q4-R3 — Governance overhead → unauthorized workarounds.** Trigger: teams bypass the pipeline to meet deadlines. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (untracked durable mutations). Mitigation: infra-level enforcement (no write path outside the pipeline); measure overhead; fund the pipeline as programme cost. Owner: Systems/programme.

### 7. Confidence
**7/10** on governance requirements (normative and defensible); **4/10** on scalable deletion-closure certification (empirically and legally unresolved).

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** on admission-as-governance-atom; **No-Go** on any claim of certifiable deletion closure.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
A completed deletion receipt is issued while the target remains reliably recoverable or activatable through a known derivative. Stop: any such case halts the release and forces redesign of the coverage gate.

## Q5 — Longitudinal evaluation, safety retention, and assurance

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
Evaluate a trajectory of complete system manifests, prequentially before each event and retrospectively after every stage. Endpoint accuracy cannot distinguish acquisition failure, destructive interference, recoverable access loss, retrieval failure, grounding drift, calibration collapse, policy refusal, or plasticity loss. A candidate metric catalogue is governed by a preregistered metric-retention rule; negative metric-ablation results are published. Reset/stateless, frozen full-context, strong temporal RAG, and periodic retraining are mandatory controls or declared substitutes. Safety, privacy, tenant isolation, and deletion are non-compensable gates; evaluation logs are governed retained state.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** trajectory manifests; prequential/retrospective testing; protocol tuples; mandatory controls; independent-harness audit; governed eval logs.
- **Narrow:** the metric catalogue to an empirically retained set; multi-site reproduction to load-bearing claims rather than arbitrary point-estimate matching.
- **Downgrade:** "Continual Calibration" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23987), "Hidden Forgetting" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02020), "MemDelta" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29914) to recent, benchmark-specific, partly single-author preprints with no universal thresholds.
- **Delete:** final-score sufficiency; universal correlation/pass thresholds; pooling numeric ranges across pretraining, editing, memory, agent experience, multimodal learning, and unlearning; composite scores without justified normalization.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes failures are separable and individually diagnosable; real failures co-occur and interact.
- Category error: the metric-retention rule is presented as empirical but presupposes a seeded-failure catalogue that is itself a design choice — metrics can only be retained for failures you thought to seed.
- Hidden assumption: hidden evaluation remains contamination-resistant over multi-year runs (listed unresolved).
- Assumes "evaluation logs as governed state" is implementable without becoming a self-referential storage/deletion burden.
- Category error: trajectory-vs-checkpoint is a spectrum; periodic manifests can miss within-window failure.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The complete-manifest protocol is unaffordable and prescriptive to paralysis; reset/stateless controls are often impossible in production; and the metric-retention rule could strip metrics that catch rare-but-catastrophic failures simply because they did not fire in the seeded set.
**Rebuttal:** The packet refuses universal thresholds and requires preregistration plus negative-result publication, converting "over-engineering" into an evidence question. Rare-catastrophe coverage is precisely why retention decisions are made pre-results and why safety/privacy gates sit outside the retention rule entirely (they are gates, not metrics). Control impossibility is handled by mandatory substitution disclosure and matched-budget reporting rather than perfect matching.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: explicitly mark safety/privacy/tenant-isolation/deletion as non-retention-eligible gates that cannot be pruned by the metric-retention rule, and require the seeded-failure catalogue to be published before evaluation.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q5-R1 — Metric-retention rule prunes a rare-failure detector.** Trigger: a metric shows no signal in the seeded set and is dropped; a rare failure later fires it. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (silent regression). Mitigation: preregistered seeds including red-team seeds; cheap always-on shadow set; independent review of retention decisions. Owner: Evaluation lead.
- **Q5-R2 — Reset/stateless control silently substituted.** Trigger: counterfactual unreconstructible; teams downgrade without disclosure. Likelihood: High. Impact: Medium (decisive comparison silently weakened). Mitigation: mandatory control-substitution declaration; missing-control disclosure as a publication-eligibility condition. Owner: Research/replication lead.
- **Q5-R3 — Hidden-evaluation contamination over long runs.** Trigger: eval set leaks into training/replay/memory; scores inflate. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High (false assurance). Mitigation: rotating hidden sets; contamination audits; access-controlled eval logs; independent-site eval for load-bearing claims. Owner: Evaluation/releases governance.

### 7. Confidence
**8/10** on trajectory-native evaluation (calibrated as confidence in the principle); **5/10** on any minimal metric set or universal threshold (honestly low).

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** for building the reconstructible multi-harness protocol; **No-Go** for any universal pass/fail threshold.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
Across long randomized and fault-seeded streams, final-score-only evaluation reproduces the same rankings and detects the same deployment-relevant incidents as the complete-manifest protocol. Stop: if it does, drop the expensive protocol; if it does not, the protocol is justified.

## Q6 — Integrated 2026–2030 research programme

### 1. Strongest version of the current answer
Commensuration before architectural commitment, around one killable hypothesis: a governed substrate-placement controller can outperform a preregistered set of strong fixed-substrate controls on trajectory utility and assurance under a common resource envelope and fully disclosed lifecycle cost. Five conditional phases — single-site subtractive gate (2026 H2); reference infrastructure and independent reproduction (2026–27); mechanism/placement tournaments (2027–28); reversible consolidation (2028–29); governed autonomy research (2029–30). No stage advances on utility alone; standardization is one enabling investment, not the sole bottleneck.

### 2. Retain / narrow / downgrade / delete
- **Retain:** commensuration first; open evidence operations; staged gates; negative-result publication; fixed-substrate kill condition.
- **Narrow:** multi-site work to tiers surviving subtraction; standardization to one enabling investment; autonomous adaptation to a gated research prototype.
- **Downgrade:** the 2029–30 autonomy phase to a research objective, not a production forecast; post-2028 consolidation economics to unknown.
- **Delete:** standardization as the sole bottleneck; fixed cost envelopes or unsupported numeric success thresholds; open components as an integrated mature system; certified CIFAR/MNIST unlearning as LLM evidence; production readiness by a fixed 2030 date.

### 3. Hidden assumptions and category errors
- Assumes the controller is a coherent entity capable of "outperforming" when the controller is itself an unvalidated mechanism choice at this stage.
- Category error: "commensuration before commitment" presumes commensuration is achievable, yet Q5 shows the metric set is unsettled — commensuration may be open research, not a gating phase.
- Hidden assumption: single-site ablation generalizes to multi-tenant production (listed unresolved).
- Assumes independent reproduction is available (compute, access, incentives) — a funding assumption, not an epistemic one.
- Category error: gating later phases on reproduced marginal value presumes the gate can be operated without the very infrastructure the programme must build.

### 4. Strongest counterargument and rebuttal
**Counterargument:** The programme's uncertainty is so pervasive (metric set unsettled, counterfactuals unreconstructible, governance heavy, economics unknown) that conditional gating likely means only the 2026 H2 ablation happens; a four-year plan that is really a one-experiment plan, trivially killable by an inadequate first harness.
**Rebuttal:** Making later phases conditional on evidence is exactly correct under this uncertainty; over-funding an unvalidated hierarchy is the larger risk. The killable-hypothesis framing is a feature — the programme should be trivially killable if simpler fixed substrates match it. The F1 gate is cheap by design (minimum instrumented hierarchy), so even a one-experiment outcome is high-information at low cost relative to the alternative.

### 5. Hardened answer for publication
As stated, plus: a post-F1 decision memo defining what evidence, at what statistical strength, advances the programme to phase 2, so "conditional" is operationally defined rather than aspirational.

### 6. Three question-specific risks
- **Q6-R1 — Inadequate first harness triggers a false kill (or false advance).** Trigger: underpowered ablation; wrong stream mix; cheap controls. Likelihood: Medium–High. Impact: High (abandoning a good line or forgiving a bad one). Mitigation: preregistration with statistical power; independent design review; pilot harness on a known-failure scenario first. Owner: Evaluation/replication lead.
- **Q6-R2 — Independent reproduction unavailable in practice.** Trigger: no independent team/site can reproduce load-bearing claims; compute/access/motivation shortfalls. Likelihood: High. Impact: Medium–High (claims stall or advance on same-team robustness alone). Mitigation: budget reproduction sites; open infrastructure; pre-negotiated replication partners; defined fallback decision rule. Owner: Programme leadership.
- **Q6-R3 — Economics/ownership/adoption bind the plan.** Trigger: legal residual-risk standards, compute access, or adoption fail to materialize. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium (schedule slip or scope change). Mitigation: quarterly monitoring of binding constraints; separate research and deployment gates; keep fixed-substrate fallback viable. Owner: Programme director.

### 7. Confidence
**6/10** on sequencing (calibrated as confidence that measurement → subtraction → tournaments → consolidation → autonomy is the least-wrong ordering, not that the programme succeeds); lower on post-2028 economics, adoption, and autonomous-write viability.

### 8. Recommendation
**Conditional Go** — authorize the harness and single-site ablation; **No-Go** on placement and consolidation infrastructure until reproduced marginal value.

### 9. Decisive experiment with falsifiable stop condition
Temporal RAG, governed periodic rebuilding, or another fixed substrate repeatedly matches the controller on utility, calibration, grounding, safety, deletion coverage, rollback, and lifecycle cost — or no independent laboratory reproduces the load-bearing results. Stop: cancel placement and consolidation investment.

## Red-Team Review of Controls

- **Privacy.** Intake captures consent scope and legal basis (good), but replay corpora and derivative stores multiply copies of personal data; retention schedules per legal basis and deletion drills are mandatory, and shadow-testing data is itself retained state subject to the same regime. Risk if exercised through Q2-R1/Q4-R2.
- **Poisoning.** AgentPoison (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12784) shows memory/KB poisoning is cheap and targeted; quarantine-plus-corroboration is a screening pipeline whose adversary model is untested. Screening is a preferred hypothesis, not a security guarantee; needs an independent detection layer, canaries, and post-admission audits. Risk if exercised through Q4-R1.
- **Provenance.** C2PA 2.2 binds origin, not truth; signed manifests are only as strong as key management and the manifest-verification path. Provenance for replays and derivatives is itself a coverage problem — a source-identity error already occurred in OpenCode's raw Round-3 delta table and was corrected under the canonical ledger, proving the control can fail. That correction is provenance, not an empirical dispute.
- **Deletion.** The ledger-plus-qualified-receipt shape is right, but the catalog is assumption-complete by design; receipts must state catalog scope and method, must include adversarial-discovery drills, and must not be marketed as legal erasure. Risk if exercised through Q4-R2 — the decisive falsifier.
- **Rollback.** Whole-bundle atomic rollback is correctly required and registry aliases are correctly rejected; but drills are listed, not demonstrated — cadence, coverage, and drill outcomes must be a release gate, not an aspiration.
- **Safety retention.** Non-compensable gates are the right structure; the residual risk is that post-update re-qualification becomes a rubber-stamp (Q2-R2) or that the metric-retention rule prunes a rare-failure detector (Q5-R1). Safety gates must be outside the retention rule and requalification must be audited against a preregistered decision tree.

**Overall disposition: Conditional Go** — fund trajectory-native measurement, governed admission, derivative lineage, deletion/rollback drills, and one preregistered single-site subtractive ablation; **No-Go** for the heterogeneous hierarchy, autonomous shared-weight mutation, mechanism rankings, and consolidation infrastructure. Every later phase is contingent on reproduced marginal value and passed non-compensable gates, under the fixed-substrate kill condition.
