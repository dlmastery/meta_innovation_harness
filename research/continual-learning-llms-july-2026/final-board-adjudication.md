## C. Consensus, dissent, and controller interpretation

### C.1 Shared board position

All five boards recommend a Conditional Go whose common operational content is narrow:

1. fund trajectory-native measurement and complete-system manifests;
2. treat deployment experience as untrusted and require governed admission before durable mutation;
3. build derivative lineage, qualified deletion reporting, and complete-bundle rollback instrumentation;
4. run one preregistered, bounded, single-site subtractive architecture test before multi-site or production infrastructure;
5. retain strong temporal RAG and governed periodic rebuilding as first-class controls or substitutes;
6. prohibit utility from compensating for safety, privacy, tenant-isolation, poison-persistence, deletion, or rollback failure;
7. require independent-team or independent-site reproduction before recent load-bearing results determine architecture or later-stage funding.

This agreement is useful as evidence that the controller package can be interpreted consistently under several review lenses. It is not evidence that the underlying scientific propositions have been independently reproduced.

### C.2 Material dissent

The most important dissent concerns the weakest question. The provisional synthesis had earlier described a four-to-one Round-2 split in which four contenders identified Q2 and Claude identified Q3. The accepted adversarial boards sharpen the distinction:

- **Q2 is the weakest empirical ordering.** No common-protocol evidence establishes a universal ranking among replay, regularization, protected adapters, sparse updates, expansion, editing, policy optimization, dense tuning, or periodic rebuilding.
- **Q3 is the weakest immediate architectural commitment.** A multi-tier placement stack could acquire funding, interfaces, and organizational inertia before its candidate tiers demonstrate marginal value.

The controller therefore does not choose one concern and discard the other. It preserves the Q2 null result and makes Q3 the first capital-allocation gate. F1 subtraction precedes any broad Q2 mechanism tournament.

Claude’s report adds higher-information dissent on four matters that the controller endorses:

1. the temporal-retrieval evidence presupposes a trusted temporal knowledge base and must not silently transfer to a store populated from unquarantined deployment traffic;
2. candidate tiers may overlap, making single-tier subtraction uninterpretable unless content overlap is measured and pairwise removals are available;
3. the Q5 metric catalogue is a research agenda until retention testing and statistical error control produce a smaller protocol;
4. the five-year roadmap should distinguish funded commitments from contingency sketches.

The controller does **not** endorse Claude’s claim that all poisoning evidence in the ledger can be summarized as “non-adaptive” without source-by-source qualification. It does endorse the narrower, decision-relevant conclusion: the packet does not validate the proposed admission pipeline against a white-box adaptive adversary who knows its rules, thresholds, and promotion conditions.

### D.2 Q1 — Scope, taxonomy, and evidence boundary

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** Use two operational labels. **Parametric continual learning** means durable mutation of weights or adapters. **System-level continual adaptation** means durable mutation elsewhere in the deployable system, including retrieval indexes, memory, persistent prompt state, skills, routers, tools, or policy. Under either label, a publishable learning claim requires an experience-caused, attributable state change that persists beyond an explicitly defined request boundary and produces reproducible held-out improvement over appropriate reset/stateless and frozen full-context controls. Authorization is a release requirement, not proof that learning occurred.

The negative evidence boundary is narrowed: the reviewed corpus does not document a frontier system that jointly demonstrates durable acquisition, bounded resources, safety retention, provenance, privacy, derivative-aware deletion, and coherent whole-bundle rollback. This is not a claim about all private systems or an exhaustive literature search.

Before external publication, the package must add the corpus-selection method, state its non-systematic limitations, define edge cases around session stitching and persistent caches, and require caching, contamination, leakage, environment drift, and control-substitution disclosures.

### D.3 Q2 — Plasticity, forgetting, and interference

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** The evidence does not support a universal mechanism ranking. Replay has broad protocol coverage and remains a reference arm where retention is lawful; it is not a universal winner. Replay sampling rate does not bound retained-corpus size. Orthogonal and sparse methods show finite-protocol benefits, expansion exchanges interference for growth and routing costs, evaluated editors degrade over long edit sequences, and importance regularization has narrow positive LLM evidence. Plasticity-loss evidence at 5M–314M non-embedding parameters does not establish frontier behavior.

The proposed constraint-keyed update service is retained as an operating hypothesis and safety policy, not as an empirical answer. Any publication must say that no accepted study evaluates that selector against strong fixed policies. The selector must enter F4 as its own arm and emit an auditable decision record: stream identity, mechanism selected, resource budgets, requalification scope, revocation path, and rollback target. Every parameter update suspends inherited assurance claims until the affected bundle has been requalified.

### D.4 Q3 — State placement and architecture

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** Heterogeneous placement is an ablatable risk-control hypothesis, not standing architecture. Direct comparative support is limited to context controls, trusted versioned temporal retrieval against tested editors, and one narrow feedback-memory result. General episodic memory, signed skills or adapters, sparse modules, and consolidation remain unpriced candidate tiers.

Temporal retrieval is therefore a **control and low-irreversibility incumbent**, not an evidence-backed universal winner. Its strongest comparative source assumes a trusted temporal knowledge base; a store populated from unquarantined deployment experience is outside that support.

F1 must be amended before execution. It must measure cross-tier content overlap; permit paired removals where redundancy makes single subtraction ambiguous; compare trusted temporal RAG, deployment-stream-populated temporal RAG under admission controls, and governed periodic rebuilding; and report utility, safety and assurance, deletion and derivative coverage, attribution, revocation, routing, and lifecycle cost separately. A tier may be useful for assurance despite no average utility gain, or increase utility while failing an assurance gate. Neither outcome may be hidden in a composite score.

### D.5 Q4 — Admission, privacy, provenance, deletion, and rollback

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** Deployment experience is untrusted evidence, not training material by default. Durable mutation requires capture, classification, quarantine, verification or corroboration, shadow testing, least-irreversible adequate placement, signed release, lineage, monitoring, qualified deletion reporting, and whole-bundle rollback.

Provenance records asserted origin and history; it does not establish truth, consent, authorization, or harmlessness. A correctly signed poisoned record remains poisoned. Deletion “closure” is narrowed to measured coverage over declared, enumerable derivative classes under a documented residual-risk standard. A receipt must distinguish removed records, rebuilt components, disabled routes, inaccessible material, probes performed, and residual risk. It is an engineering artifact, not by itself a legal determination under GDPR Article 17.

Before any production admission path, the programme must conduct a white-box adaptive red-team exercise. It must also test the unresolved interaction between rollback and deletion: restoring a pre-event bundle must not silently restore data or derivatives previously deleted. External use of a deletion receipt requires legal review, and F1/F5 must price governance cost per admitted event against periodic rebuilding.

### D.6 Q5 — Longitudinal evaluation and assurance

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** The strongest scientific result in the package is that optimization loss, free-form recall, endpoint accuracy, grounding, calibration, coverage, recoverability, and benchmark ranking can diverge. Endpoint accuracy cannot establish acquisition, retention, correct grounding, calibration, or release safety.

Evaluation must follow trajectories of complete signed manifests, prequentially and retrospectively. Reset/stateless, frozen full-context, temporal-RAG, and periodic-retraining controls remain required where available; any substitution must be named and treated as a confound. Safety, privacy, tenant isolation, deletion, and rollback remain non-compensable gates.

The broad metric catalogue is not yet the protocol. It is a candidate set. Before F1 begins, a provisional assurance set must be preregistered and frozen. F2 must add primary endpoints, multiple-comparison control, replication of metric-retention decisions, and a held-out or exploratory failure reserve so the protocol is not optimized only for failures its designers anticipated. Evaluation logs are governed retained state and must have access, contamination, retention, and deletion controls.

### D.7 Q6 — Programme structure and investment sequence

**Controller-endorsed conclusion.** Fund commensuration before architecture around a killable hypothesis: a governed placement controller may outperform strong fixed-substrate alternatives on trajectory utility and assurance under common resource ceilings and complete lifecycle costing.

The publication must distinguish commitments from possibilities. The currently authorized commitment is Stage 1: the amended F1 subtractive gate plus the minimum F2 instrumentation needed to interpret it. Stage-2 reference infrastructure and reproduction preparation may receive conditional enabling funds, but later mechanism tournaments, consolidation, learned routing, autonomous durable-write proposals, and deployment-shaped economics remain contingency sketches. Their calendar labels are planning placeholders, not forecasts or evidence of likely programme survival.

A named replication mechanism, funded access, or equivalent credible independent-site plan must exist before load-bearing findings are promoted. Same-team second-family or second-harness work remains robustness testing, not independent reproduction.

## G. Architecture and investment impact

The controller’s architectural posture changes materially.

First, there is no approved six-tier target architecture. The only authorized implementation is a disposable, minimally instrumented test stack sufficient to run F1. Context, temporal retrieval, episodic memory, skills or adapters, sparse modules, and consolidation are experimental arms or subtraction targets, not layers to preserve.

Second, temporal RAG is retained as a strong control and low-irreversibility incumbent for mutable facts only where the store’s trust and admission conditions are explicit. Governed periodic rebuilding remains equally important and may become the final architecture if it matches utility and assurance more cheaply.

Third, no shared-base-weight online mutation path is authorized. Dense consolidation, if ever reached, remains offline, lineage-complete, rebuildable, fully requalified, and subordinate to coherent whole-bundle rollback and deletion-residual standards.

Investment is correspondingly narrowed:

- **Fund now:** minimal manifest recording; protocol and resource instrumentation; versioned temporal-store control; derivative ledger; deletion and rollback drills; provisional assurance set; adaptive red-team preparation; F1 and the necessary portion of F2.
- **Conditionally prepare:** portable harnesses, open mixed streams, hidden evaluation, and independent reproduction arrangements.
- **Do not fund now:** production hierarchy, learned placement platform, long-horizon module-expansion infrastructure, consolidation platform, autonomous durable-write serving, or a deployment programme tied to 2030.
- **Preserve the exit:** if fixed temporal RAG or periodic rebuilding wins, stopping controller and consolidation investment is a successful programme outcome.

## I. Reversal evidence

Evidence that would justify a broader **Go** includes:

- a preregistered F1 result in which at least one predeclared non-RAG tier shows reproducible marginal value against its own absence on utility or an independently reported assurance endpoint under common ceilings and complete lifecycle costing;
- independent-team or independent-site reproduction of that result;
- a pruned controller occupying a reproducible Pareto frontier over temporal RAG and periodic rebuilding across model families and harnesses;
- adaptive red-team evidence that admission materially reduces durable poison placement relative to direct ingestion and repair, at acceptable cost;
- accurate qualified deletion receipts surviving adversarial derivative canaries under a declared residual standard;
- a production-validated substitute for reset/stateless controls benchmarked against true reset in a laboratory setting;
- consolidation reducing lifecycle cost without failing deletion, rollback, safety, or tenant-isolation gates.

Evidence that would require **No-Go** or termination includes:

- no tier remaining load-bearing after amended subtraction;
- temporal RAG or governed periodic rebuilding matching the stack at equal or lower lifecycle cost;
- cross-tier overlap making the proposed architecture uninterpretable by feasible ablation;
- inability to establish coherent lineage or restore a complete pre-event bundle;
- a known derivative remaining recoverable after a completed receipt;
- rollback restoring deleted information;
- persistent quarantine escape, cross-tenant disclosure, or unrecoverable safety regression;
- governance overhead eliminating net lifecycle value;
- failure to obtain independent reproduction of load-bearing results;
- endpoint-only evaluation detecting the same consequential incidents and yielding the same decisions as the expanded protocol, which would reverse the case for the latter’s additional expense.

## J. Prohibited deployment pathways

Until reversed by the evidence above, the following pathways are prohibited:

1. direct ingestion of user, tool, or agent experience into shared durable state without quarantine, authorization, and staged evaluation;
2. autonomous shared-base-weight mutation in serving;
3. autonomous writes to shared memory, adapters, routers, tools, or policies outside shadow mode and explicit authorization;
4. temporal retrieval populated from unquarantined deployment experience while cited as if supported by trusted-KB evidence;
5. cross-tenant retrieval, replay, summaries, logs, adapters, or promotion;
6. replay retention without purpose, legal basis, expiry, provenance, poison controls, and derivative-aware deletion;
7. learned routing without tenant and valid-time checks, calibrated abstention, an explicit safe fallback, and route-conditioned monitoring;
8. parameter release that inherits prior safety approval without requalification;
9. unbounded sequential editing using editor families already shown to degrade in evaluated long-sequence protocols;
10. consolidation without a lineage-complete rebuildable epoch, pre-consolidation manifest, deletion test, and atomic bundle rollback;
11. source-row deletion presented as derivative-aware deletion;
12. behavioral suppression presented as causal erasure or automatic GDPR Article 17 compliance;
13. semantic-memory rollback or registry alias reassignment presented as complete-system rollback;
14. deletion receipts described as complete, perfect, guaranteed, certified, or legally dispositive under the current evidence;
15. evaluation logs retained outside the privacy, contamination, access, and deletion regime;
16. release after any safety, privacy, tenant-isolation, poison-persistence, deletion, or rollback gate fails, regardless of average utility;
17. architecture or production claims resting on a single fresh preprint, same-team robustness check, or five-board shared-packet agreement;
18. external claims that the hierarchy is established, that one mechanism universally wins, or that production maturity will occur by a fixed 2030 date.

## K. Final controller decision

### Decision: **Conditional Go**

**Go now** for:

- the two-label taxonomy as measurement and publication discipline;
- the evidence-backed conclusion that endpoint metrics can diverge from recall, grounding, calibration, and recoverability;
- minimal complete-manifest and resource instrumentation;
- governed admission and lineage as design requirements, without claiming validated adaptive security;
- qualified deletion and complete-bundle rollback instrumentation, without claiming achieved closure;
- the amended F1 single-site subtraction gate and the necessary F2 metric-pruning work.

**No-Go now** for:

- adopting the heterogeneous hierarchy as standing architecture;
- representing the constraint-keyed selector as a validated method;
- a broad mechanism tournament before F1 and the provisional assurance protocol are ready;
- production admission before adaptive red-teaming;
- multi-site architecture infrastructure;
- consolidation infrastructure;
- autonomous shared-state or shared-weight mutation in serving;
- externally represented certified deletion;
- funded or forecast production stages through 2030.

The programme advances beyond Stage 1 only if at least one tier survives amended subtraction, matched-resource comparisons show a Pareto advantage, load-bearing results receive independent reproduction, every non-compensable gate passes, and lifecycle value remains positive after governance and incident costs. Otherwise, the controller must select the simpler fixed substrate, most plausibly strong temporal RAG or governed periodic rebuilding.

## Closure ruling

**The listed publication edits have been made:** corpus-absence claims are limited to the reviewed public corpus; evidentiary consensus/convergence claims are prohibited; the selector and hierarchy are unvalidated hypotheses; temporal retrieval is bound to its trusted-store precondition; the metric catalogue is provisional; Stage 1 is separated from later contingencies; the amended F1/F2 prerequisites are integrated; and source-status and protocol-scope qualifiers remain adjacent to load-bearing claims. The package is therefore publication-ready as a **Conditional-Go research and falsification programme**, not as evidence for a validated production architecture.
