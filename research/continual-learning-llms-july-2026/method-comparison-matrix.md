# Continual-Learning Method Comparison Matrix and Decision Guide

**Evidence boundary:** Public primary and official evidence available by **2026-07-31 23:59 UTC**.  
**Interpretive boundary:** “Parametric continual learning” means durable mutation of weights or adapters. “System-level continual adaptation” includes durable changes to retrieval indexes, episodic memory, persistent prompts, skills, routers, tools, or policy. Request-local context alone is not durable learning.

## Decision rules

### 1. Establish whether durable learning occurred

A publishable continual-learning claim requires:

1. an authorized and attributable state change caused by an ordered experience stream;
2. persistence beyond the current request;
3. reproducible held-out improvement over matched reset/stateless and frozen full-context controls; and
4. a declared substrate, stream regime, resource envelope, and reversibility class.

Request-local prompting is not durable learning. Persistent retrieval, prompt summaries, or memory may qualify as **system-level continual adaptation**, but must not be represented as weight learning.

### 2. Match the substrate to the change

| Change or obligation | Provisional decision rule |
|---|---|
| Volatile, correctable, or time-indexed facts | Start with versioned temporal RAG |
| Repeated tenant-specific experience with validated feedback | Test isolated persistent feedback memory against static RAG and full context |
| Recurring procedure requiring lower inference overhead | Test a signed, revocable adapter or module; retain retrieval as the control |
| Bounded factual correction | Compare temporal RAG with a logged, checkpointed editing arm |
| Broad domain or capability acquisition not achievable through external state | Consider replay-supported adapter or dense updating under offline release controls |
| Policy or tool-behavior change | Treat as a distinct RL/policy update with shadow evaluation and independent safety gates |
| Replay prohibited by law, consent, or storage constraints | Compare regularization, protected adapters, sparse regions, and rebuilding; do not treat missing replay as a methodological defect |
| Sensitive experience with strong erasure obligations | Prefer addressable, rebuildable external state; avoid dense consolidation unless deletion residuals and rebuild economics pass |
| Low reuse, contradiction, poison discovery, or expired authorization | Quarantine, demote, retire, or rebuild rather than promote |
| Repeatedly validated, high-reuse material | Consolidate only if lifecycle savings exceed continued retrieval and revocation remains credible |

“Least irreversible adequate mechanism” is a safety-motivated operating policy, **not an experimentally proven universal ordering**.

### 3. Govern replay as retained state

Replay is the lawful-retention reference arm when permitted. Every comparison must disclose:

- retained-corpus size separately from replay sampling rate;
- consent, purpose, expiry, and tenant restrictions;
- poisoning and conflict controls;
- optimizer and schedule handling;
- deletion propagation into all dependent artifacts; and
- storage, evaluation, and rebuild costs.

A replay percentage alone says nothing about buffer size, privacy burden, or lifecycle feasibility.

### 4. Treat every parameter update as a new release

After any adapter, sparse, editing, dense, or policy update:

1. suspend reliance on inherited assurance claims;
2. test acquisition, retention, calibration, grounding, safety, privacy, tool policy, and tenant isolation;
3. evaluate access failure separately from destructive erasure;
4. preserve the complete pre-update manifest;
5. release through staged canaries; and
6. roll back the whole deployable bundle on a hard-gate failure.

Reduced parameter drift is insufficient if the system fails to acquire the new capability or loses future plasticity.

### 5. Require promotion and demotion to be two-sided

Promotion from retrieval or episodic state into adapters, sparse regions, or dense weights requires:

- repeated validated reuse;
- complete source and derivative lineage;
- benefit in shadow evaluation;
- lifecycle savings over continued retrieval;
- passed safety and privacy requalification; and
- a successful revocation drill.

Contradiction, declining reuse, poison discovery, routing failure, consent withdrawal, or assurance regression must trigger quarantine, demotion, retirement, or rebuilding.

### 6. Keep periodic rebuilding as a first-class substitute

Periodic rebuilding is not merely a baseline to defeat. It should be selected when:

- continuous mutation has no reproducible marginal utility;
- controller or routing complexity creates avoidable incidents;
- replay or derived state cannot be governed lawfully;
- deletion requires corpus-level exclusion and reconstruction;
- module growth or retirement exceeds the resource envelope; or
- governance and incident costs eliminate the apparent acquisition advantage.

### 7. Test the controller subtractively before building architecture

The first controller experiment should compare:

- the minimum instrumented placement stack;
- the stack minus episodic memory;
- minus adapters or signed skills;
- minus sparse modular state;
- minus consolidation;
- temporal-RAG-only; and
- governed periodic rebuilding.

Delete every tier whose removal causes no reproducible loss. Do not fund a multi-site architecture programme until at least one non-RAG tier survives this test.

### 8. Compare trajectories, not final checkpoints

Required controls are:

- reset/stateless;
- frozen full-context;
- strong temporal RAG; and
- periodic rebuilding or retraining.

Evaluation should cover acquisition, maximum degradation, recoverability and recovery cost, future plasticity, calibration, abstention, grounding, stale answers, routing, poison persistence, safety, privacy, tenant isolation, state growth, deletion residuals, rollback coherence, and lifecycle cost. Expanded metrics should be retained only when they detect a distinct seeded failure or change a decision.

### 9. Use Pareto decisions and non-compensable gates

Compare arms under declared ceilings for optimization compute, inference tokens or tool calls, and peak and cumulative persistent state. Itemize screening, human review, routing, evaluation, retirement, rebuilding, deletion, incidents, latency, energy, and operator burden.

Do not collapse these dimensions into an unsupported composite score. Higher utility cannot compensate for failure of safety, privacy, tenant isolation, poison-persistence, deletion, or coherent-rollback gates.

### 10. Advance only on reproduced marginal value

A method or controller should advance only when:

1. a cheaper subtractive test leaves it load-bearing;
2. it occupies a reproducible Pareto frontier under matched resources;
3. it survives same-team second-model-family and second-harness robustness checks;
4. an independent team or site reproduces every load-bearing result before standing architecture or funding commitment;
5. all hard assurance gates pass; and
6. lifecycle value remains positive after governance, deletion, and incident costs.

Otherwise, choose the simpler fixed substrate—currently most plausibly temporal RAG or governed periodic rebuilding.

## Explicit non-rankability caveats

1. **No universal ordering is supported.** Replay, regularization, adapters, sparse updates, expansion, editing, dense tuning, policy optimization, rebuilding, and consolidation have not been compared under one common protocol spanning facts, skills, procedures, preferences, agent experience, policy, multimodal streams, and unlearning.

2. **Cross-paper numbers are not commensurate.** Studies differ in model family and size, task sequence, task-identity assumptions, stream order, replay legality, metric definition, checkpoint, inference budget, persistent-state budget, and lifecycle accounting. Their percentages, backward-transfer values, compute ratios, and latency claims must not be assembled into a leaderboard.

3. **Lower forgetting can mean weaker acquisition.** Reduced drift or near-zero backward transfer does not establish successful learning, adequate transfer, retained safety, or sufficient future plasticity.

4. **Behavioral loss is not automatically erasure.** Prompt recovery and rapid relearning can indicate access failure or re-keying; stable accuracy can conceal grounding or calibration drift. Loss, recall, accuracy, grounding, calibration, and recoverability must remain separate.

5. **External state is not intrinsically safe or reversible.** Retrieval and memory improve addressability but introduce poisoning, leakage, staleness, context competition, routing failures, and derivative proliferation. Deleting a row does not delete its embeddings, summaries, caches, traces, replay descendants, adapters, router policies, or consolidated weights.

6. **Adapter and module isolation is finite-protocol evidence.** Orthogonality, sparsity, reserved capacity, expansion, and routing do not guarantee indefinite capacity or no forgetting. They may exchange shared-parameter interference for growth, collision, routing, and retirement costs.

7. **Editing evidence is method- and sequence-specific.** Long-sequence degradation in tested editors does not prove that every future editor must fail; local successes do not establish an indefinite factual-update system.

8. **Plasticity-loss scaling is bounded to tested sizes.** Results from models up to the evaluated non-frontier scale cannot be treated as proof of frontier-model behavior, in either direction.

9. **Deletion claims are substrate-specific.** Legal erasure, source-record deletion, semantic rollback, behavioral suppression, whole-bundle rollback, and causal influence removal are different propositions. Current evidence does not justify “complete,” “perfect,” “guaranteed,” or “certified” deletion closure for general LLM continual learning.

10. **Evidence status matters.** Several important 2026 results are recent, single-study preprints or workshop papers. They are admissible as scoped evidence but should not determine standing architecture or funding without independent reproduction. Same-team second-family or second-harness work is a robustness check, not independent replication.

11. **Synthesis agreement is not empirical replication.** Agreement among analyses derived from a shared evidence pack increases synthesis consistency only.

12. **Lifecycle costs remain incompletely priced.** General episodic memory, signed skills, protected adapters, sparse modules, learned routing, and consolidation remain unpriced architectural tiers unless compared with their own absence and with temporal RAG and rebuilding under full lifecycle accounting.

13. **The placement controller is a hypothesis, not the winner.** No accepted primary study demonstrates that the complete heterogeneous hierarchy outperforms temporal RAG or periodic rebuilding on trajectory utility, assurance, reversibility, and total lifecycle cost.

14. **Absence of public evidence is not an impossibility theorem.** The corpus does not publicly document an indefinitely learning frontier system that jointly satisfies bounded resources, durable acquisition, safety retention, provenance, privacy, derivative-aware deletion, and coherent whole-bundle rollback. It does not establish that no private system exists.

15. **The current decision is procedural, not a ranking:** use temporal RAG as the provisional mutable-fact control, preserve periodic rebuilding as a first-class substitute, test every additional tier subtractively, and authorize irreversible mutation only after reproduced marginal value and non-compensable assurance gates.
