## Controller posture

### Funding authorization

**Stage 1 is the only funded commitment:** the amended F1 single-site subtractive gate and the minimum F2 instrumentation required to interpret it. Reference-harness and independent-reproduction preparation may receive conditional enabling funds. F3–F12 and the 2027–2030 dates are contingency sketches and planning placeholders, not authorized architecture, forecasts, or evidence that the programme will survive Stage 1.

The programme tests—not assumes—the central hypothesis:

> **A governed substrate-placement controller can outperform a preregistered set of strong fixed-substrate controls on trajectory utility and assurance under a common resource envelope and fully disclosed lifecycle cost.**

The hierarchy is **not** treated as the winner. The ordering constraint is:

1. **First:** a cheap, single-site subtractive ablation capable of killing unnecessary tiers.
2. **Only if that passes:** a matched-resource mechanism and placement tournament.
3. **Only after replicated Pareto advantage and hard assurance gates:** reversible consolidation and autonomous write proposals.

Reset/stateless, frozen full-context, strong temporal RAG, and periodic rebuild/retraining are mandatory controls or substitutes throughout. Safety, privacy, tenant isolation, deletion, and coherent whole-bundle rollback are non-compensable gates.

### Common protocol rules

- **Evaluation object:** trajectory of signed, complete-system manifests: model, adapters, indexes, memories, prompts, tools, router, and policy.
- **Streams:** report fact, skill, procedure, agent-experience, policy, multimodal, and unlearning streams separately; randomize order and declare task identity/boundaries.
- **Primary matched-resource envelope:** optimization FLOPs, inference token/tool-call budget, and peak plus cumulative persistent bytes.
- **Separately itemized lifecycle costs:** acquisition, screening, human review, storage, routing, evaluation, retirement, rebuild, deletion, incident response, latency, energy, and engineering/operator time. A result is not “matched” if these are silently omitted.
- **Core metrics:** prequential acquisition; forward/backward transfer; maximum degradation; recoverability and recovery cost; fixed-budget future plasticity; calibration, coverage, abstention, grounding; temporal consistency and stale-answer rate; router and memory failures; poison persistence; safety/privacy/tool-policy regression; tenant isolation; state growth; deletion residuals; rollback coherence; total lifecycle cost.
- **Metric-retention rule:** keep an expanded metric only if it reproducibly detects a distinct seeded failure or changes a decision relative to a simpler protocol.
- **Stage-1 assurance freeze:** before confirmatory F1 data are exposed, preregister and freeze primary utility and assurance endpoints, multiplicity/error control, control substitutions, and an untouched held-out or exploratory failure reserve.
- **Threshold discipline:** non-inferiority margins, minimum effects, confidence intervals, sample sizes, and stop boundaries must be preregistered from pilots. They are design choices—not claims derived from the current literature.
- **Pilot-and-freeze discipline:** each confirmatory work package begins with a separate pilot stream and seeds used only to estimate variance, feasibility, margins, sample sizes, and stop boundaries. The analysis plan and thresholds are then frozen before untouched confirmatory streams are revealed.
- **Resource fairness:** compare arms under common ceilings and report actual FLOPs, bytes, latency, utilization, and full lifecycle cost. Never add dummy computation or storage merely to force numerical equality; use Pareto analysis where substrates consume different resource mixes.
- **Automatic stop gates:** quarantine escape into serving, cross-tenant disclosure, prohibited real-PII use, unrecoverable safety regression, knowingly incomplete deletion receipt, or failure to restore a coherent pre-event bundle.

# Twelve flagship work packages

## 2026 H2 — Kill cheap assumptions before building infrastructure

### F1. Single-site bounded subtractive placement ablation — **first programme gate**

- **Hypothesis:** At least one tier beyond versioned temporal retrieval provides repeatable within-site marginal trajectory utility or assurance under a common resource ceiling.
- **Competing arms:**  
  1. Full minimally instrumented placement stack built from disposable reference stubs, not production infrastructure.  
  2. Minus isolated episodic memory.  
  3. Minus signed skills/revocable adapters.  
  4. Minus sparse modular state.  
  5. Minus consolidation.  
  6. Trusted versioned temporal-RAG-only.  
  7. Temporal RAG populated from an admitted deployment stream under the F5 governance controls.  
  8. Periodic rebuild from governed corpus.  
  The predicted load-bearing tier must be named before results are seen.
- **Stream/data:** One bounded, synthetic-plus-public mixed stream containing corrections, supersessions, recurring procedures, conflicting facts, tenant boundaries, coordinated poison, expiry, and deletion requests. No real PII.
- **Model scope:** One open 7B–14B-class instruction model; frozen base except in the consolidation arm.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Before implementation begins, record absolute numerical caps for total engineering hours, wall-clock duration, and lifecycle spend; F1 authorization requires each cap to remain below the corresponding approved F4 setup-and-run ceiling. Also preregister common inference-token/tool-call, persistent-byte, and optimization-FLOP ceilings and report actual utilization. Stubs must reuse one manifest, ledger, and resource meter, must not be productionized, and must itemize router, evaluation, deletion, rollback, and operator costs.
- **Identifiability:** Measure cross-tier content overlap before subtraction. Where overlapping or redundant tiers make a single removal ambiguous, preregister paired removals and do not infer that either tier lacks value from a masked single-subtraction result.
- **Metrics:** Report separately—never as one compensating score—prequential and retrospective utility; safety and assurance; calibration and grounding; stale answers; attribution and revocation; route-conditioned failure; state growth; poison persistence; deletion and known-derivative coverage; rollback coherence; latency; governance cost per admitted event; and full lifecycle cost.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Tenant namespace tests, poison quarantine, derivative lineage, post-deletion retrieval/trigger/membership/behavioral probes, and whole-bundle rollback drill.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:**  
  - **Falsifier:** Removing every unevidenced tier causes no reproducible loss outside the preregistered non-inferiority margin, or temporal RAG/periodic rebuild matches the full stack at lower lifecycle cost.  
  - **Stop:** Any hard assurance breach or inability to trace a test item’s derivatives.
- **Dependencies:** Minimal reusable manifest recorder, temporal store, lineage ledger, resource meter, and disposable tier stubs only—no multi-site platform. Pilot and confirmatory streams/seeds are disjoint.
- **Expected decision:** Delete non-load-bearing tiers. If no tier survives, stop hierarchy and consolidation investment and standardize subsequent work around temporal RAG and periodic rebuild.
- **Evidence tested:** Q3/Q6 central placement hypothesis; E007–E010, E034, E056, E063; Round-2/3 claim that memory can relocate interference and may not beat ICL/RAG.

### F2. Trajectory protocol and metric-pruning challenge

- **Hypothesis:** Full-manifest trajectory evaluation detects consequential failures or rank changes that endpoint accuracy and conventional continual-learning metrics miss.
- **Competing arms:**  
  1. Endpoint-score-only evaluation.  
  2. Conventional acquisition/BWT/FWT protocol.  
  3. Full-manifest protocol with calibration, grounding, recoverability, routing, poison, deletion, rollback, and cost.  
  Identical systems and seeded faults are evaluated under each protocol.
- **Stream/data:** Controlled streams seeded with inaccessible-but-recoverable knowledge, destructive interference, calibration drift, grounding drift, stale retrieval, router misselection, poison persistence, incomplete deletion, and inconsistent rollback.
- **Model scope:** Same model/system used in F1; include frozen retrieval and one adapter mutation arm.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Identical system trajectories and fault seeds; separately report evaluation FLOPs, tokens, storage, human review, and wall-clock burden.
- **Confirmatory design:** Before confirmatory trajectories are exposed, freeze the provisional Stage-1 assurance set, primary endpoints, family-wise or false-discovery error control, and a disjoint held-out/exploratory failure reserve. Replicate metric-retention decisions on independent seeded-failure runs rather than retaining metrics because they fire once.
- **Metrics:** Primary incident recall/precision and decision changes; secondary detection delay, ranking changes, false alarms, and marginal evaluation cost per independently detected failure. Report the declared multiple-comparison procedure and exploratory findings separately.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Synthetic identifiers only; evaluation logs receive retention, access, and deletion policies and are included in closure drills.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:**  
  - **Falsifier:** Endpoint-only evaluation catches the same incidents and yields the same rankings across randomized streams within the preregistered margin.  
  - Drop any expanded metric that never fires independently across replicated seeded-failure runs.
- **Dependencies:** F1 manifests and minimal harness; fault-seeding catalogue.
- **Expected decision:** Freeze a minimal sufficient protocol for 2027; eliminate metric theatre before multi-site export.
- **Evidence tested:** Q1/Q5 measurement-first thesis; E002, E053, E054, E057, E058, E062.

## 2027 — Reproduce load-bearing claims, then run the matched tournament

### F3. Second-family reproduction of load-bearing 2026 claims

- **Hypothesis:** Three programme-shaping findings survive a second model family and independent implementation: poor durable factual-write recall despite training success, useful frozen-weight feedback memory in a recurring domain, and calibration degradation missed by accuracy.
- **Competing arms:**  
  - Factual writes: direct sequential writes versus diverse restatement and retrieval controls.  
  - Feedback memory: frozen static RAG versus persistent validated feedback memory versus full-context.  
  - Calibration: continual tuning with versus without calibration-aware replay/control.
- **Stream/data:** Reconstructed protocols plus a preregistered transfer stream; preserve original and transfer results separately.
- **Model scope:** A second open model family distinct from the original studies, initially 7B–14B; no claim of frontier transfer.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Match per-subexperiment optimization FLOPs, inference tokens, and persistent bytes; itemize feedback distillation, retrieval, evaluation, and storage.
- **Metrics:** Acquisition, free-form recall, prompt recovery, intrusion, recurrent-task reward, calibration/coverage, accuracy, grounding, and cost.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Synthetic facts and consent-cleared feedback; lineage and deletion across raw records, summaries, indexes, and adapters.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** A load-bearing finding fails if direction does not reproduce or the original arm is non-superior under the preregistered equivalence/non-inferiority test. Stop using that claim to justify architecture if it fails both replication and transfer.
- **Dependencies:** F2 protocol; public preregistration package.
- **Expected decision:** Promote only replicated findings into programme assumptions; demote failures to paper-specific observations.
- **Evidence tested:** E051, E062, E063; also E053’s loss-without-recall warning.

### F4. Matched-resource mechanism and placement tournament — **allowed only after F1**

- **Hypothesis:** Mechanism choice may be constraint-dependent; the constraint-keyed selector is an unvalidated policy hypothesis, not an empirical result. No universal ranking is assumed, while one or more fixed arms may occupy a reproducible Pareto frontier.
- **Competing arms:** Reset/no durable state; frozen full-context; temporal RAG; persistent feedback memory; replay plus rewarming/gradient alignment; EWC; ordinary and orthogonal/protected adapters; sparse protected regions; modular expansion; bounded editing; dense tuning; periodic rebuilding; the explicit constraint-keyed selector with auditable decision records; rule-based controller; learned controller. If F1 eliminated a tier, it stays excluded unless explicitly reintroduced as a falsification check.
- **Stream/data:** Separate randomized fact, skill/domain, recurring procedure, multi-tenant experience, and replay-prohibited streams. Do not pool their numeric results.
- **Model scope:** Two open model families at comparable scale; one frozen-weight and one weight-changing configuration per applicable arm.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Tournament cells share optimization-FLOP, inference-token/tool, and persistent-byte ceilings. Report replay storage separately from replay rate. Itemize routing, retirement, screening, evaluation, deletion, rebuild, incidents, latency, and energy.
- **Metrics:** Acquisition/retention, max degradation, recoverability, future plasticity, calibration, grounding, safety, router error, module growth, deletion/rollback, and Pareto lifecycle cost.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** No unscreened feedback writes; replay corpus governed as retained state; every update re-earns safety/privacy/tool-policy qualification; tenant crossing, poison persistence, or failed rollback halts the cell.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:**  
  - **Controller falsifier:** Any fixed arm matches the controller on utility and assurance within margin at lower lifecycle cost across both families.  
  - **Constraint-keyed-policy falsifier:** One simple mechanism stably dominates across all regimes, both model families, and preregistered stream replications.  
  - **Stop:** Unbounded module growth without validated retirement or hard assurance failure.
- **Dependencies:** F1 Go/Conditional-Go, F2 metric set, F3 replication status.
- **Expected decision:** Produce regime-specific selection rules, not a global mechanism hierarchy; identify which arms merit 2028 capacity/consolidation work.
- **Evidence tested:** Q2/Q3; E001, E003, E005, E019–E035, E059–E061, E070–E074. Directly tests the audits’ “no universal ranking” conclusion.

### F5. Governed admission versus direct ingestion and post-hoc repair

- **Hypothesis:** Liability-controlled admission reduces attack consequence and deletion gaps enough to justify its utility, latency, and reviewer cost.
- **Competing arms:**  
  1. Direct ingestion.  
  2. Direct ingestion plus post-hoc unlearning/repair.  
  3. Governed temporal memory admission.  
  4. Full lineage-governed admission with quarantine, corroboration, shadow testing, least-irreversible placement, and staged release.  
  5. Periodic rebuild from admitted corpus.
- **Stream/data:** Synthetic multi-tenant stream with legitimate feedback, duplication, conflicts, stale facts, white-box adaptive and non-adaptive poison, malicious promotion, consent withdrawal, and deletion requests. The adaptive arm exposes admission rules, thresholds, and promotion conditions to the attacker.
- **Model scope:** Frozen temporal-RAG system plus one revocable-adapter system; no shared base-weight online updates.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Equal accepted-event opportunity and inference budget; report screening compute, reviewer minutes, quarantine delay, rejected useful events, derivative scans, repair/rebuild, and incident cost.
- **Metrics:** Acquisition, false rejection, detection rate/delay, attack consequence, reviewer burden, time-to-release, poison persistence, closure coverage, residual influence, and rollback restoration.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Synthetic identities; quarantine escape and cross-tenant retrieval stop the run. No serving admission path is authorized before the white-box adaptive arm passes its preregistered gate. Receipts must enumerate removed, rebuilt, disabled, and residual components and receive legal review before external compliance use.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** Governed admission is falsified if direct ingestion plus periodic rebuild is non-inferior on utility and attack consequence, achieves equal or better closure, and costs less. Stop if known poison reaches serving or a receipt omits a known derivative.
- **Dependencies:** F2 fault library and manifest/lineage schema; independent security review.
- **Expected decision:** Choose admission depth by measured marginal risk reduction; reject governance steps that add no distinct protection.
- **Evidence tested:** Q4; E014–E018, E043–E046, E055–E056, E067, E079.

## 2028 — Test long-horizon capacity, routing, and reversible consolidation

### F6. Long-horizon plasticity and capacity-saturation study

- **Hypothesis:** Within the preregistered horizon and resource envelope, protected adapters, sparse regions, or expansion exhibit measurable deterioration in plasticity, routing, state growth, or lifecycle cost; model scale alone does not establish immunity.
- **Competing arms:** Replay-supported dense tuning; EWC; orthogonal adapters; sparse reserved regions; expansion; replay-free task-free CPT; periodic rebuild.
- **Stream/data:** Long randomized multilingual/domain and procedural sequences with recurring and conflicting tasks, plus replay-lawful and replay-prohibited variants.
- **Model scope:** At least three open-model scales spanning the feasible range; conclusions remain bounded to tested sizes and must not be extrapolated to frontier systems.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Common cumulative optimization-FLOP and persistent-byte ceilings with equal evaluation cadence; report actual utilization rather than padding. Track replay corpus size separately from sample rate and report router, retirement, checkpoint, and rebuild costs.
- **Metrics:** Fixed-budget acquisition, future learning speed, BWT, recovery, capacity occupancy, router collision, module growth, calibration, safety retention, and cost per accepted capability.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Public/consented corpora only; requalification after every update; replay deletion and module retirement drills.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** The finite-horizon deterioration hypothesis fails for a method if future plasticity and assurance remain stable without state growth or increasing cost over the complete preregistered horizon and resource envelope. This result does not establish indefinite immunity. Stop an arm on irreversible safety drift or growth beyond its resource envelope.
- **Dependencies:** F4 identifies viable arms and resource envelopes.
- **Expected decision:** Establish retirement/rebuild triggers and bounded scale claims; determine whether expansion is merely deferred saturation.
- **Evidence tested:** E005, E022–E028, E037, E052, E059–E061, E070, E073–E074.

### F7. Rule-based versus learned placement and abstaining router

- **Hypothesis:** A learned controller earns its complexity only if it improves placement under drift without increasing stale, unsafe, or cross-tenant routing relative to explicit rules.
- **Competing arms:** Fixed temporal RAG; explicit least-irreversible placement rules; learned router; learned router with calibrated abstention/human review; oracle placement as an analysis-only upper bound.
- **Stream/data:** Mixed tenant, temporal fact, recurring procedure, conflict, expiry, poison, and workload-shift stream, including held-out substrate and query shifts.
- **Model scope:** Frozen base plus retrieval, episodic, skill/adapter, and optional sparse tiers surviving F1/F4.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Common inference-token/tool and persistent-byte ceilings with actual utilization reported; include router training, inference, review, retraining, misroute incident, and retirement costs.
- **Metrics:** Routing accuracy, entropy, abstention, route-conditioned utility/safety, stale-answer rate, tenant isolation, poison propagation, latency, and lifecycle value over fixed rules.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Mandatory abstention when tenant, consent, lineage, or valid time is unresolved. Any cross-tenant route stops the arm.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** Learned placement is falsified if explicit rules are non-inferior on utility and superior on any hard assurance or cost dimension. Stop if silent unsafe-routing rate crosses the preregistered bound.
- **Dependencies:** At least one non-RAG tier survives F1 and F4; F5 admission controls.
- **Expected decision:** Authorize learned routing, retain explicit rules, or eliminate placement automation.
- **Evidence tested:** Q3 controller claim; E008–E013, E022, E024, E034, E057, E074.

### F8. Rebuildable consolidation, revocation, and retirement tournament

- **Hypothesis:** Consolidating repeatedly reused experience can reduce recurring inference/storage cost without losing deletion, rollback, or safety assurance relative to continued retrieval or modular retention.
- **Competing arms:** No consolidation/temporal RAG; retained revocable adapters; sparse consolidation; adaptive consolidation; dense offline consolidation; periodic rebuild from governed corpus.
- **Stream/data:** Repeated validated procedures and stable domain material interleaved with later contradiction, consent withdrawal, poison discovery, and module-retirement events.
- **Model scope:** One or two F4-winning families; base-weight changes occur only in offline rebuildable epochs.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Common optimization-FLOP, inference, and persistent-byte ceilings over the full trajectory with actual utilization reported; include rebuild, lineage, evaluation, retirement, deletion, and incident costs.
- **Metrics:** Utility, inference savings, retained calibration/grounding/safety, interference, recovery, derivative count, deletion residuals, rollback coherence, retirement success, and lifecycle break-even.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Checkpoint-plus-manifest before consolidation; full safety requalification; post-deletion retrieval/trigger/membership/behavioral probes. No “certified deletion” claim.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** Consolidation is falsified if retrieval or periodic rebuild is non-inferior on utility and cheaper after lifecycle costing, or if any consolidated arm cannot meet the residual-risk deletion standard. Stop on poison survival after receipt or incoherent rollback.
- **Dependencies:** F4 Pareto arms, F5 admission pipeline, F6 retirement triggers.
- **Expected decision:** Permit only forms of consolidation with replicated lifecycle advantage and revocation evidence; otherwise retain external/rebuildable state.
- **Evidence tested:** E005, E009, E016–E018, E031–E034, E056, E069, E072, E079. E069 informs theory only, not LLM empirical guarantees.

## 2029 — Independent replication and bounded autonomous write proposals

### F9. Independent two-site controller-versus-fixed-substrate replication

- **Hypothesis:** Any controller advantage found in 2027–28 reproduces under an independent implementation, alternative harness, randomized orders, and a second deployment-shaped stream.
- **Competing arms:** Best fixed temporal RAG; periodic rebuild; best fixed parametric/modular arm from F4; pruned rule controller; learned controller only if F7 passed.
- **Stream/data:** One common open hidden stream and one independently designed site-local stream, both containing corrections, skills, tenant boundaries, poison, deletion, and tool use.
- **Model scope:** Two model families; identical protocol tuple where possible, with site-specific implementation differences explicitly preserved.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Common budget envelopes and auditable meters; itemize site labour, infrastructure, evaluation, incidents, deletion, and rebuild.
- **Metrics:** Within-site Pareto fronts, cross-site direction consistency, rank reversals, incident detection, calibration/grounding, assurance-gate results, and lifecycle cost.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Independent stop authority at each site; synthetic or consent-cleared data; successful deletion and bundle-rollback drills required.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** The controller hypothesis fails if a fixed substrate matches it at lower cost at either site and that result reproduces, or if controller advantage reverses across harnesses without an identified protocol-tag explanation.
- **Dependencies:** Positive F4 and relevant F7/F8 result; exportable F2 protocol and manifests.
- **Expected decision:** Replicate and advance, narrow to a specific regime, or stop controller investment.
- **Evidence tested:** Q1/Q5/Q6 replication requirements; E003, E007, E057–E058; the audit warning that shared-pack agreement is not replication.

### F10. Governed autonomous write proposals in shadow mode

- **Hypothesis:** Agents can propose useful durable mutations while authorization, abstention, review, canary, and rollback keep assurance failures below the manually curated pipeline at acceptable cost.
- **Competing arms:** No durable writes; human-authored writes; agent proposals with mandatory review; agent proposals with risk-tiered review and abstention; fully automatic proposals as a non-serving red-team arm only.
- **Stream/data:** Sandboxed recurring tool-use and support workflows with delayed outcomes, misleading feedback, adversarial instructions, tenant separation, and later corrections/deletions.
- **Model scope:** Frozen base and revocable external/modular state; no autonomous shared-base-weight mutation.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Equal task/inference budgets; account for proposal generation, review time, abstention, canaries, monitoring, rollback, and incident investigation.
- **Metrics:** Validated reuse, proposal precision, harmful-write rate, abstention quality, reviewer burden, time-to-benefit, safety/tool-policy drift, poison persistence, deletion, and incident-adjusted lifecycle value.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Shadow-only initially; no production actuation; dual authorization for shared promotion; immediate stop on quarantine escape, tenant crossing, or unauthorized tool/policy mutation.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** Autonomous proposals are falsified if they do not improve validated reuse or lifecycle value over human-only writes, or increase hard-gate incidents. Any unauthorized durable mutation stops the run.
- **Dependencies:** F5 admission, F7 routing if used, F9 replicated platform result.
- **Expected decision:** Continue shadow research, allow bounded canaries, or terminate autonomous-write work.
- **Evidence tested:** Q4/Q6 autonomous-governance hypothesis; E004, E012–E015, E020, E052, E063–E065.

### F11. Adversarial deletion-closure and whole-bundle rollback board

- **Hypothesis:** Qualified deletion receipts and atomic manifests can bound—not eliminate—residual influence across all known derivatives under adaptive attack.
- **Competing arms:** Record deletion only; semantic-memory rollback; post-hoc unlearning; rebuild from governed corpus; derivative-aware deletion plus full-bundle restore. Each restore arm must execute deletion first and then restore a pre-event bundle to test whether rollback resurrects the deleted record or any known derivative.
- **Stream/data:** Canaries propagated deliberately into records, embeddings, indexes, summaries, caches, traces, replay corpora, adapters, router policies, and consolidated weights; adaptive triggers and reconstruction attempts.
- **Model scope:** Retrieval-only, adapter, sparse, and consolidated systems surviving earlier gates.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Equal attack/probe budgets; report rebuild compute, downtime, derivative scanning, human/legal review, and incident cost.
- **Metrics:** Known-derivative coverage, residual retrieval/trigger/membership/behavioral influence, restoration hash/dependency consistency, utility after deletion, rebound, and receipt accuracy.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Synthetic canaries only; external red-team stop authority; no receipt may say complete, perfect, guaranteed, or certified unless a future applicable standard actually supports that wording.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** A method fails if a receipt issues while a known derivative remains recoverable above the declared residual standard, rollback restores inconsistent components, or restoration resurrects deleted state or derivatives. Repeated failure stops the associated substrate from later-stage use.
- **Dependencies:** F5 lineage, F8 consolidation variants, adversarial-board protocol.
- **Expected decision:** Define substrate-specific receipt language, residual standards, and prohibited substrates for sensitive experience.
- **Evidence tested:** E016–E018, E043–E046, E055–E056, E067, E069, E079; Q4 distinction among legal erasure, behavioral unavailability, and causal removal. E069 contributes theory only: its experiments use CIFAR/MNIST with CNN/ResNet models and provide no empirical LLM deletion or unlearning guarantee.

## 2030 — Final external-validity and investment decision

### F12. Deployment-shaped lifecycle economics and controller kill trial

- **Hypothesis:** The surviving controller delivers a replicated Pareto advantage over strong fixed substrates under realistic workload, review, deletion, and incident conditions.
- **Competing arms:** Best fixed temporal RAG; periodic rebuild; best fixed modular/parametric arm; final pruned controller. Governed autonomous proposals are included only if F10 passed.
- **Stream/data:** Long, replayable, deployment-shaped but bounded multi-tenant stream with seasonality, recurring work, sparse high-value updates, conflicts, policy changes, deletion requests, poison campaigns, tool failures, and outages.
- **Model scope:** Two independently operated open-model systems; no claim of generality beyond tested families and workloads.
- **Matched resource accounting:** Common service-level and lifecycle budget envelopes. Measure training, inference, storage, routing, review, evaluation, retirement, rebuild, deletion, downtime, incidents, latency, energy, and operator burden.
- **Metrics:** Trajectory utility and validated reuse; calibration, grounding, abstention; safety/privacy/isolation; state growth; deletion/rollback; availability; incident-adjusted cost; and Pareto dominance—not a composite score.
- **Safety/privacy/deletion gates:** Independent safety board; staged canaries; no open-ended shared-weight mutation; immediate rollback on hard-gate failure; qualified deletion receipts.
- **Preregistered falsifier/stop:** The central programme hypothesis is rejected if any fixed arm repeatedly matches the controller on utility and assurance at lower lifecycle cost, if controller advantage fails independent reproduction, or if governance overhead erases net value. Stop deployment-shaped expansion on any unrecoverable hard-gate incident.
- **Dependencies:** F9 replication; F10/F11 results; external ownership and legal review.
- **Expected decision:**  
  1. **Go:** standardize a narrowly scoped controller.  
  2. **Conditional-Go:** retain only the proven regime/tier.  
  3. **No-Go:** standardize temporal RAG or periodic rebuild and close controller/consolidation work.
- **Evidence tested:** Q6 central hypothesis and kill condition; E003, E007–E010, E034, E057, E063. No accepted source validates controller lifecycle economics; that remains the programme hypothesis under test.

## Controller decision rule

The programme should not advance because a complex arm has higher average utility. It advances only when:

1. the cheaper subtractive test leaves at least one tier load-bearing;
2. matched-resource comparisons show a Pareto advantage;
3. the result survives same-team second-family robustness checks and reproduction by an independent team or site;
4. safety, privacy, tenant isolation, deletion, and rollback gates all pass; and
5. lifecycle value remains positive after governance and incident costs.

Otherwise, the controller should choose the simpler fixed substrate—most plausibly strong temporal RAG or periodic rebuild from a governed corpus.
